# Singability (唱感分析)

Lyrics exist to be sung. Word choice affects how comfortable a line is to vocalize, especially at emotional peaks. This file provides singability guidance.

## Vowel openness and pitch

Vowels have different "openness" — how wide the mouth opens to produce them. Open vowels project better on high notes; closed vowels feel constrained.

### Mandarin vowel openness scale

| Openness | Vowels | Feeling | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Very open | a, ai, ao | Expansive, powerful | Chorus climax, held notes |
| Open | an, ang, ou | Warm, resonant | Chorus, emotional peaks |
| Medium | e, ei, en, eng | Neutral | Verses, transitions |
| Closed | i, ü, in, ing | Tight, intimate | Quiet verses, whispered lines |
| Very closed | u, un | Dark, restrained | Low-energy passages |

### Cantonese vowel openness

| Openness | Vowels | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Very open | aa, aai, aau | 花/開/愛 | Chorus climax |
| Open | ai, au, oi | 世/手/來 | Emotional peaks |
| Medium | e, ei, ou | 些/飛/走 | Verses |
| Closed | i, ui, eui | 詩/回/追 | Quiet passages |
| Very closed | u, yu | 路/雨 | Low-energy |

## Rules of thumb

### 1. Chorus peak = open vowels

The highest emotional moment (usually the hook line or the last line of the chorus) should end with an open vowel. This lets the singer "open up" on the climax.

- ✅ "我在这里等你回**来**" (ai — open)
- ❌ "我在这里等你回**去**" (ü — closed, feels swallowed)

### 2. Avoid consonant clusters at speed

Fast passages need smooth consonant flow. Avoid:
- zh/ch/sh + r combinations in rapid succession
- Multiple stops (b/p/d/t/g/k) in a row without vowels between

### 3. Held notes need sustained vowels

Characters on long held notes should have:
- Open or medium vowels (not closed)
- No checked tones in Cantonese (入声 ends in -p/-t/-k, can't sustain)
- Avoid nasal endings (-n, -ng) on very long holds (the nasal closes the sound)

### 4. Line-final singability

The last character of each line gets the most vocal weight. Prioritize:
- Open vowels for emotional lines
- Rhyme-compatible vowels (don't sacrifice rhyme for openness)
- Avoid 轻声 (neutral tone) characters at line ends

### 5. Breath points

Singers need to breathe. Lines longer than ~12 characters (Mandarin) or ~10 characters (Cantonese) should have a natural pause point (a comma, a phrase break) where the singer can catch breath without breaking meaning.

## Scoring (optional)

When reviewing a completed lyric for singability:

- Check the chorus hook line's final vowel: open = +1, closed = -1
- Check held-note positions (if known): open vowel = +1, checked tone = -2
- Check for breath points in long lines: present = +1, missing = -1
- Sum across the song. Positive = good singability. Negative = revise.

## Application in workflow

- **While writing**: prefer open-vowel words at emotional peaks, especially line-final positions in the chorus
- **Self-check**: after writing, scan chorus line endings — if more than half are closed vowels, swap where possible without breaking meaning
- **Don't over-optimize**: singability is one factor among many. A perfect image with a closed vowel beats a generic image with an open vowel.
